Accessibility Service

EdgePad uses the Android AccessibilityService API for its core features. With your permission, EdgePad:

  • Shows the edge handles, the floating button, and the pad over other apps.
  • Reads the name of the app on screen when you switch apps. EdgePad uses it to show your recently used apps in the pad (Recent apps folder), to stay out of the way in apps you exclude, and to sort app lists by how often you use each app. The usage count and last-used time are saved in EdgePad's database on your device.
  • Reads what's on screen only when you run a Paste action, to find the text field you're typing in and paste into it.
  • Passes a tap on a handle through to the app underneath, and performs the system actions you add to the pad: Home, Back, Recent apps, notifications, quick settings, lock screen, screenshot, all apps, and the power menu.

EdgePad does not send information obtained through the AccessibilityService API to us or to any third party. Analytics events record only which type of pad item was used, never app names or screen content. If Android backup is on, the usage counts above may be included in your device's Google backup, like other app data.

You can turn off EdgePad in your device's Accessibility settings at any time. Uninstalling EdgePad deletes the data saved on your device.

Notification Access (optional)

The controls pad can show what's playing. For this, Android requires notification access. With it, EdgePad reads the song title, artist, album art, and the app that's playing, only to show them in the controls pad. EdgePad doesn't read your notifications and never saves or sends song information. Without this access, the media buttons still work; the song details just aren't shown. You can turn it off in your device's notification access settings at any time.

Google Drive Backup (optional)

EdgePad can back up your setup to your own Google Drive. It stays off until you connect Google Drive in Settings › Backup & restore. If you turn on auto backup, EdgePad backs up once a day.

A backup includes:

  • Your pads, folders, and favorites, and the list of items you've added or edited, with what's in them: app shortcuts, website links, text saved for Paste actions, and icons, including any picture you chose as an icon.
  • For contacts you added to a pad: the name, phone number, and a small copy of the contact photo. Your contact list itself is not included.
  • Your handles, the apps you excluded, and your EdgePad settings.
  • Your device's name (for example, “Galaxy S24”), the EdgePad version, and a random ID that EdgePad creates so each device keeps its own backup.

App usage counts are not included.

The backup is saved in a hidden app folder in your Google Drive. Only EdgePad can open it, and it doesn't appear among your Drive files. EdgePad asks only for access to this folder (the drive.appdata permission), so it can't see or change your other Drive files. The backup goes straight from your device to Google over an encrypted connection and is never sent to us. Google stores it under the Google Privacy Policy.

You can delete a backup at any time. In Settings › Backup & restore, open Manage Drive › Delete this device's backup, or delete any device's backup from the list in Restore from Drive. Disconnecting stops backups on this device but keeps the backups already in your Drive. Uninstalling EdgePad doesn't delete them either. You can also delete them in Google Drive on the web (Settings › Manage apps › EdgePad › Delete hidden app data). If you remove EdgePad's access in your Google Account settings, the backups may also be deleted.
