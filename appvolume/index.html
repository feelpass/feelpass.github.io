Every app at its own volume.
Yours comes back when you leave.

AppVolume is an Android app that switches your media volume to the level you set for each app when that app comes on screen, and puts your previous volume back when you leave. Per-app volume and mute are free with no limit on apps (with ads); AppVolume Pro, a one-time purchase, removes ads and adds category rules and more.

It's not a mixer for two apps playing at once. Android has one media volume, and AppVolume sets it for whichever app is on screen.

What AppVolume does

Mute your games, keep videos low, and use another level on Bluetooth. AppVolume does the volume-button dance every time you switch apps, so you don't have to.

One volume per app

Set a media volume for each app, from 0 to 100% in 1% steps. It kicks in when the app comes on screen, and your previous volume comes back when you leave. Set it to 0% to mute the app, and it holds while a video stays open in a picture-in-picture window.

When you press the volume keys in an app

Choose whether AppVolume ignores the change, remembers it as the new volume, or locks the app to its preset. Set it once for every app, or app by app.

Another level for Bluetooth, headphones or night

Conditional rules give an app a different volume on the speaker, wired headphones or Bluetooth, or during hours you choose. When more than one rule matches, the one on top wins. Drag to reorder.

All your games at once

Category rules in Pro set one volume each for Games, Video, Social, Music & audio, News, Maps & navigation, Photos and Productivity, including apps you install later. With My categories, you group your own apps under one volume. One is free.

Pause when you need to

Pause everything, or a single app, for 5 minutes up to 6 hours, or until you turn it back on. The Auto volume tile in Quick Settings pauses and resumes it in one tap.

A quick on-screen notice

When a volume is applied or restored, a short notice shows at the top or bottom of the screen. Its menu lets you adjust the volume, pause, or stop managing that app.

AppVolume is a good fit if you

  • reach for the volume buttons every time you open a game or a short-video app. Apps you've set mute themselves when they open, and your volume comes back when you leave.
  • want a different level on Bluetooth earbuds or a speaker than on your phone speaker, and something quieter at night
  • want to try it for free first. Per-app volume and mute are free, with no limit on apps.

AppVolume isn't for you if you

  • want to mix two sounds at once, like loud music behind a quiet game. AppVolume changes the one media volume, so while a muted game is on screen, the music behind it goes quiet too. If your phone has a per-app volume mixer built in, that may suit you better.
  • need to change ringtone, notification or alarm volume, or go louder than 100%. AppVolume changes media volume only, from 0 to 100%.
  • use an iPhone, or don't want to turn on accessibility access. AppVolume needs Android 10 or later, and with its accessibility service off, the volume won't change.

Questions people ask

Is there an app to control volume per app on Android?

AppVolume does exactly that. Set a media volume for each app, and it switches to that level when the app comes on screen, then goes back to your previous volume when you leave. No root needed, and no persistent notification.

Can I keep music playing and turn down just the game?

No. AppVolume can't mix two apps playing at the same time. Android has one media volume, so while a muted game is on screen, music playing in the background goes quiet too. If your phone has a per-app volume mixer built in, it may be the better fit for this.

Can it mute games automatically when I open them?

Yes. Set the game's volume to 0% and it's muted every time you open it, with your volume back when you leave. That's free, for as many apps as you like. To cover games you install later too, turn on category rules in Pro. They follow the category each app declares, so apps that don't declare one need to be added by hand.

Can I use a different volume with Bluetooth earbuds, or a lower one at night?

Yes. Conditional rules set a different volume for the speaker, wired headphones or Bluetooth, or for hours you choose. Time rules are checked when you open the app, so if the hour changes while you're using it, the new volume applies the next time you open it. You get 2 conditional rules for free.

Is AppVolume free? How many apps can I use it with?

Yes. Per-app volume, mute, volume key modes, pause and the Quick Settings tile are free, with no limit on apps. The free limits are 2 conditional rules (app and category rules combined) and 1 custom category. The free version shows banner, app-open and full-screen ads.

Can I get rid of the ads? Is Pro a subscription?

Yes, AppVolume Pro removes all banner, app-open and full-screen ads, and it's a one-time purchase, not a subscription. It's US$4.99 in the US, and prices vary by region. Pro also unlocks category rules, unlimited conditional rules and custom categories, and settings backup and restore.

Why does AppVolume need accessibility access?

It needs to know which app is on screen to apply that app's volume. Android doesn't let ordinary apps see that, so AppVolume uses its accessibility service for this one job. If the service is off, the volume won't change, and AppVolume sends a notification when it gets turned off.

Does AppVolume read my screen or send my app names anywhere?

It doesn't read text on your screen or what you type, and it doesn't send app names or package names. The accessibility service checks only the package name and window class of the active window and whether a picture-in-picture window is open, and it never taps or types for you. Usage stats (Firebase Analytics) include things like the app type Android assigns (for example, game), the volume level and the output type. Ads (AdMob), crash reports (Crashlytics) and purchase checks (Google Play) also use the internet.

What happens if I change the volume with the volume keys inside an app? Can I lock it?

You choose: Ignore, Remember or Lock. Ignore is the default and keeps your preset after you leave, Remember saves the new level as the preset, and Lock puts the volume back to the preset even while you're in the app. Set it for all apps at once or app by app.

Does it change ringtone or alarm volume?

No, only media volume. Ringtone, notification and alarm volumes stay as they are.

Can I move my settings to a new phone?

Yes. With Pro's backup and restore, export your settings to a file and load it on the new phone. The backup file is saved wherever you choose. There's no account or login; your settings are stored on your device.

Is AppVolume on iPhone?

No. AppVolume runs only on Android 10 and later. There's no iPhone version.
