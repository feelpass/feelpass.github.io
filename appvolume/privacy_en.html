Privacy Policy

AppVolume - Per-App Volume Control

In short: AppVolume has no account or login, and your per-app volume settings and rules are stored on your device. The app shows ads through Google AdMob (no ads are shown while Remove Ads is active; see section 4 for when it becomes active) and sends usage events and crash reports to Google through Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. The names and package names of the apps you configure are not sent. Instead, usage events include the app type assigned by Android (for example, game, video, or social) and volume levels, and every time the Accessibility Service detects that an app has come to the foreground and applies its volume, this information is sent to Firebase Analytics.

1. Information We Collect and Process

The information AppVolume handles is as follows.

  • Volume settings and rules — stored on your device: The app stores the following in a database on your device.
    • Per-app volume settings: for each app you configure, its package name and app name, whether the setting is on, the media volume level (%), the time a per-app pause ends, and the times the setting was created and last changed
    • Condition rules: for rules you create for an app, their conditions (output device: speaker, wired, or Bluetooth; time range: start and end time), volume level (%), whether the rule is on, and the times it was created and last changed
    • Category rules: for each app type (games, video, social, and so on), whether the rule is on, the volume level (%), and the time it was last changed
  • App settings and operating records — stored on your device: The time a global pause ends; your theme and language; how volume changed with the volume keys is handled (Ignore, Remember, or Lock); how on-screen notices are shown; whether you finished the first-run guide (onboarding); whether Remove Ads is active (true/false); from version 1.0.28, whether Remove Ads has ever been active on this device (true/false) and records for the welcome offer (the time you first opened version 1.0.28 or later on this device, and whether you dismissed the welcome offer card on the home screen); the time an interstitial ad was last shown; records used to decide when to ask for a review (the number of times a volume was applied, counted only up to 10; the time you first opened the home screen; the time a review was last requested); records used to decide when to send a service-off notification (whether the service has ever connected, the time it last connected, the time a notification was last sent, and whether notification permission has been requested); and whether each one-time usage event has already been sent. These are stored in the app's storage on your device.
  • Temporary baseline volume record — stored on your device: When you enter an app whose volume is applied, the app temporarily stores the media volume level to restore later, the output device type at that time (speaker, wired, or Bluetooth), that app's package name, and the time of the record in the app's storage on your device. This lets the original volume be restored even if the app process stops unexpectedly. See section 6 for when it is deleted.
  • Installed app information — read on your device: So you can choose which apps to configure, the app loads the package name, name, and icon of the apps shown on your home screen (launcher) from your device and displays them. For category rules and for the app type value in usage events, the app also reads the app type Android assigns to each app (ApplicationInfo.category) on your device. The loaded list is not stored or sent, and the app type is sent only as the app_category value of the usage events below; only apps you turn on or whose settings you change are saved to the volume settings above.
  • Current media volume and output device type — checked on your device: The app reads your device's current media volume to apply and restore volumes and to set the starting value when you add an app. For output device rules and restore protection, it determines only the type of the connected audio output device (speaker, wired, or Bluetooth); it does not read the names or addresses of Bluetooth devices.
  • Usage events — sent to Firebase Analytics: The app sends the following events to Firebase Analytics. App names and package names are not included in any event. The "app type" below (app_category) is the value assigned by Android (one of game, audio, video, image, social, news, maps, productivity, accessibility, undefined, other, or unknown).
    • screen_view: when you open a screen — the screen name (home, app list, volume editor, rule editor, settings, theme/language selection, category rules, category settings, category app list, custom category editor, Remove Ads, onboarding)
    • onboarding_page_view, onboarding_skipped, onboarding_completed: when you view a page of the first-run guide, when you end it with "Later", and when you finish it (including with "Later") — the page number and whether the Accessibility Service is on
    • a11y_disclosure_shown, a11y_disclosure_result: when the accessibility disclosure dialog is shown and when you agree or decline — where it was opened (home, settings, onboarding) and your response
    • enable_service_clicked: when you agree to the accessibility disclosure on the home screen or in onboarding and go to the accessibility settings (no additional values)
    • a11y_settings_abandoned: when you agreed to the disclosure and went to the settings but came back within 5 minutes without turning the service on (no additional values)
    • a11y_enabled: once, when the Accessibility Service connects for the first time (no additional values)
    • service_connected: when the Accessibility Service connects — the number of apps whose volume setting is on
    • a11y_lost: when the Accessibility Service is unbound or destroyed, or when a service-off notification is sent — the reason (unbound, destroyed, detected off)
    • managed_app_added (and first_app_added the first time): when you turn on an app in the app list or first save a new app's setting on the volume editor screen — that app's app type and where it happened
    • managed_app_removed: when you turn off an app in the app list or delete an app on the home screen — that app's app type and where it happened
    • volume_preset_changed: when a volume you changed on the volume editor screen is saved — that app's app type and the new volume level (%)
    • volume_key_mode_changed: when you change how the volume keys are handled for an app on the volume editor screen — a scope value (app) and the mode you chose (Ignore, Remember, Lock, or follow the app-wide setting). Which app it is is not sent
    • route_profile_set: when you save a new condition rule with an output device condition on the rule editor screen — the output device type and what the rule belongs to (an app, an app-type category, or a custom category)
    • schedule_set: when you save a new condition rule with a time range condition on the rule editor screen — what the rule belongs to (an app, an app-type category, or a custom category). The times are not sent
    • category_rule_changed: when you turn a category rule on or off or save a new value — the app type, whether it is on, and the volume level (%)
    • custom_category_saved: when you create or edit and save a custom category (a group of apps you pick yourself), or turn it on or off or save a new volume level for it — the number of apps in it, whether it is on, and the volume level (%). The category name and the names and package names of its apps are not sent
    • custom_category_deleted: when you delete a custom category (no additional values)
    • category_app_excluded, category_app_included: when you uncheck or re-check an app on the category app list screen and it is saved — the category kind (app-type category or custom category). Which app and which category are not sent
    • volume_applied (and first_volume_applied the first time): when the Accessibility Service applies a volume (for example, when it detects a foreground app switch, when a pause ends, when the service connects, or when the output device changes) — that app's app type, the applied volume level (%), and where the value came from (per-app setting, category rule, or custom category)
    • volume_apply_rejected: when the Accessibility Service tried to apply a volume but the device did not take the value (for example because of Do Not Disturb, the safe volume warning, or a fixed-volume device), so it left the volume unchanged (no additional values)
    • volume_restored: when the previous volume is restored — what triggered it (leaving to the home screen, switching to another app, a pause, a global pause, the service stopping, reconnecting after an unexpected stop, output returning to the speaker)
    • volume_learned: when, in Remember mode, a volume you changed in an app is saved as its setting — that app's app type and the saved volume level (%)
    • restore_skipped_route_changed: when restoring the previous volume is skipped because the output device changed (no additional values)
    • volume_lock_suspended: when, in Lock mode, returning the volume to your setting is stopped for the rest of your time in that app — the reason (the returned value did not take effect, something else changed the value right back, or the number of returns reached its limit). Which app it is is not sent
    • pause_set, pause_resumed: when you pause or resume automatic volume switching — the scope (global or app), where it happened (home, on-screen notice, Quick Settings tile), and the pause length in minutes (-1 for indefinite)
    • overlay_action: when, on a text notice, you tap the pause button, choose or cancel an item in the pause length list, open the menu, or in the menu change and save the volume, change how the volume keys are handled, or choose to open settings, turn this app off, or exclude it from its category — the action type. Which app it is and the values you changed are not sent
    • ad_shown: when an app open ad or an interstitial ad is shown — the ad format and what triggered it
    • paywall_shown: when you open the Remove Ads screen — a value for where it was opened (recorded as settings wherever you open it from)
    • purchase_initiated, purchase_completed, purchase_failed, purchase_restored: when you start, complete, or fail a Remove Ads purchase, or try to restore purchases — the product ID (remove_ads_lifetime, or from version 1.0.28 remove_ads_lifetime_welcome for the welcome offer product) and a failure reason code or whether the restore succeeded. Order numbers, amounts, payment methods, and purchase tokens are not sent.
    • backup_exported, backup_imported: when you export settings to a file or import and merge them from a file — the number of per-app settings exported or imported. The number of rules and file names, locations, and contents are not sent.
    • review_prompted: when the app requests the Google Play review dialog (no additional values)
    • User properties: the number of apps whose volume setting is on (managed_app_count), whether the Accessibility Service has been turned on (a11y_enabled), the current output device type (audio_route: speaker, wired, Bluetooth, or another external output), and whether a Remove Ads purchase was completed on this device (premium, recorded only when a purchase completes)
    For the information Firebase Analytics collects automatically alongside these events, see section 4.
  • Crash reports — sent to Firebase Crashlytics: When the app crashes, error details, device diagnostic information, and a commit hash identifying the app build (git_hash) are sent. See section 4.
  • Advertising data — collected by Google AdMob: To serve ads, the AdMob SDK may collect and use device identifiers such as the advertising ID, your IP address, and ad interaction data. In regions that require consent, such as the European Economic Area (EEA) and the UK, Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) asks for your consent and stores your choice on your device. See section 4.
  • Purchase information — handled by Google Play: Remove Ads purchases are handled by the Google Play billing system. The app does not receive or store payment methods such as card numbers, billing addresses, or payment amounts; it stores only whether Remove Ads is active (true/false) and, from version 1.0.28, whether it has ever been active on this device (true/false) on your device. See section 4.
  • Backup files — saved where you choose: When you export from the settings screen, the app creates a backup file (JSON) in a location you pick yourself with the system file picker. It also does not contain pause states, category rules, the times settings were created or changed, or account or device identifiers. The app does not send this file anywhere, and when you import, it reads only the file you pick, once.
  • Information the app does not ask for: The app does not ask you to enter a name, email address, or phone number, and does not request permissions to access your contacts, photos, storage, precise location, camera, microphone, or Bluetooth devices.

2. Accessibility Service

AppVolume uses the Android Accessibility Service for its core feature: switching to the media volume you set for an app when that app comes to the foreground, and switching back when you leave it.

  • Events it receives: The service subscribes to two event types: window state change events (TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED) and window list change events (TYPE_WINDOWS_CHANGED). Versions up to 1.0.26 subscribe only to window state change events.
  • What it reads: It reads the package name and window class name from each event, and the package name of the currently active window, to determine which app is in the foreground. From version 1.0.27, to know whether an app whose volume was applied is still on screen as a picture-in-picture window, it also reads the list of windows on screen (window type, whether it is picture-in-picture, and position) and the package name of the picture-in-picture window. The service configuration declares window content retrieval (canRetrieveWindowContent), but the only value the app's code reads from the active window and the picture-in-picture window is the package name; it does not read text shown on the screen or anything you type.
  • What it does: When an app you configured and turned on (or an app with no per-app setting that falls under a category rule you turned on) comes to the foreground, the service changes the device's media volume to your setting, and restores the previous volume when you leave that app. From version 1.0.27, while that app stays on screen as a picture-in-picture window, the volume is kept instead of restored, and it is restored when the picture-in-picture window closes. If condition rules exist, it uses the value of the rule that matches the current output device and device time. If the output device changes while you use the app, it applies the value for the new device and does not write the previous device's volume to another device.
  • On-screen notices: When it changes, restores, or remembers a volume, it briefly shows a notice on the screen. This notice is not an Android notification but a window the Accessibility Service draws over the screen (an accessibility overlay). From version 1.0.25, the default style is a small volume bar at the edge of the screen (it does not receive touches), and a text notice is shown when a volume changed with the volume keys is remembered. If you choose "Detailed" under "Notice style" in settings, text notices are shown instead, and only the notice shown when a per-app setting is applied has a pause button. In the settings screen under "On-screen notices", you can choose to show them when applied and restored, only when applied, or not at all.
  • Volume changed with the volume keys: Depending on your setting, in Remember mode (the default up to version 1.0.26) the volume you changed in an app is saved as the setting for that app (or for the condition rule applied at the time) when you leave it; in Lock mode the media volume is returned to your setting if it changes in the app; and in Ignore mode (the default from version 1.0.27) nothing happens. Only in Lock mode does the app receive Android's media volume change broadcasts. The app does not intercept or record the volume key presses themselves (it does not declare key event filtering), and all of this is processed on your device.
  • What is sent: Each time a volume is applied, that app's app type and volume level are sent to Firebase Analytics as a volume_applied event. In addition, the Accessibility Service sends volume_restored, volume_learned, restore_skipped_route_changed, volume_apply_rejected, volume_lock_suspended, service_connected (the number of apps turned on), a11y_lost, and, when you use the buttons or menu on an on-screen notice, overlay_action and pause_set events, as well as the output device type user property (audio_route) (see section 1). App names and package names are not sent. No volume_applied event is sent when you switch to an app that has neither its own setting to apply nor a category rule turned on, an app whose own setting you have turned off, or an app you have paused.
  • On-device storage: The app's code does not store a history of foreground app switches on your device. The only values the Accessibility Service writes to your device are pause states (when you choose to pause from a notice, or when a global pause period ends), volume settings saved in Remember mode, the temporary baseline volume record (section 1), the volume-applied count used for review requests, the service connection record, and whether each one-time usage event has already been sent. However, events sent to Firebase are temporarily kept on your device by the Firebase SDK until they are sent.
  • No actions on your behalf: The service does not tap, scroll, or type for you. It does not declare the gesture capability (canPerformGestures).
  • Your control: The Accessibility Service works only after you turn it on yourself in Android Settings. Before taking you to the accessibility settings screen, the app shows a disclosure dialog and asks for your consent. If you turn the service on within 5 minutes after agreeing, you are brought back to the app automatically. You can turn the service off at any time in Android Settings, and you can pause automatic volume switching from the home screen or the Quick Settings tile.

3. Permissions

  • Accessibility Service (BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE): Detects the foreground app and changes the media volume automatically (see section 2).
  • Installed app queries: To show the list of apps you can configure, the app declares that it queries apps shown on your home screen (launcher). Libraries bundled with the app also declare some query entries automatically (the ad SDK: apps that handle web links, phone calls, text messages, and adding calendar events; the Google Play Billing Library: the Google Play Store app). The app does not request the permission to query all apps (QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES).
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS, Android 13 and later): If the Accessibility Service is turned off, or is on but not connected, the app sends an "AppVolume is off" notification. Using WorkManager, the app checks the service state about every 6 hours (whether the service is connected and whether this app is turned on in Android's accessibility settings). The check itself happens on your device, and an a11y_lost event is sent when the notification is sent. It notifies you once each time the service goes off, does not notify again while it stays off, and notifies once more if the service reconnects and then goes off again. Tapping the notification opens the app's home screen. The permission is requested on the home screen while the Accessibility Service is running, or when you come back after turning the service on from the settings screen; it is asked only once across the app and not asked again if you deny it. The app does not send persistent notifications. Versions up to 1.0.27 do not send promotional notifications either. From version 1.0.28, the app sends a promotional notification: a reminder that the welcome offer is ending. When you open the app's screens, it uses WorkManager to schedule one reminder for about 24 hours before the welcome offer period ends (7 days from the time you first opened version 1.0.28 or later on this device; see section 4). The reminder is not sent if, at that time, Remove Ads is active, a lookup found that this Google account already owns a Remove Ads product, the ownership lookup has not finished, the offer period has ended, the price of the welcome offer product could not be received from Google Play, that price is not lower than the regular product's price, or notification permission is not granted. It is sent on a notification channel separate from the service-off notification ("Offers"), and tapping it opens the app's Remove Ads screen (the app does not go there while the first-run guide is in progress). The app does not ask for notification permission separately for this reminder.
  • Internet (INTERNET) and network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): Used to check ad consent and load ads, to send Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics data, and to open this privacy policy page from the settings screen.
  • Advertising ID (AD_ID) and ad services permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS): Declared by the Google AdMob SDK and Firebase Analytics for ad serving and measurement.
  • Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING): Declared by the Google Play Billing Library and used to buy Remove Ads and to confirm and restore the purchase.
  • WAKE_LOCK and install referrer access (BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE): Declared by Firebase Analytics and WorkManager. The install referrer is Google Play information about how the app was installed.
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE and DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION: Declared automatically by Android libraries bundled with the app (WorkManager, AndroidX). The app does not run a foreground service, and the latter is a permission private to this app that prevents other apps from calling receivers used only inside the app.
  • Quick Settings tile: You can add an "Auto volume" tile to Quick Settings in the notification shade. The tile only switches automatic volume switching between paused and resumed and does not request any permission (it is protected by BIND_QUICK_SETTINGS_TILE so that only the system can connect to it).

Besides not showing ads, it lifts the free usage limits. While Remove Ads is not active, you can create new condition rules only up to 2 in total (per-app and per-category rules combined) and new custom categories only up to 1, and you cannot turn on a category rule (per app type) that is off or export or import a settings backup. Condition rules and custom categories you already created, and category rules that are already on, keep working regardless of the limits and can be edited or deleted. Buying and paying is handled by Google Play, and payment information is processed by Google under Google's policies. Using the Google Play Store app on your device, the app looks up the product price and whether this Google account owns the purchase when the app process starts (including when it starts because the Accessibility Service connects), and looks up the product price when you open the Remove Ads screen, and when the payment sheet could not be opened from that screen, only if it has not been received yet (from version 1.0.28, it also looks up ownership at that point if no ownership lookup has finished yet). It looks up whether this Google account owns the purchase again when you tap Restore purchase, when you return to the app's screens, and when you tap the buy button and Google Play reports that the product is already owned. From version 1.0.28, if the app has already found that the account owns the product, tapping the buy button looks up ownership right away without opening the payment sheet. Also from version 1.0.28, it looks up ownership when the welcome offer reminder task (section 3) runs; that task also looks up the product price if it has not been received yet. When a purchase completes, the app sends the purchase information back to Google Play to acknowledge it but does not store the purchase token; it stores only whether Remove Ads is active (true/false) and, from version 1.0.28, whether it has ever been active on this device (true/false) on your device.
  • Welcome offer and when Remove Ads is active (from version 1.0.28):

5. How Information Is Used

  • Settings and records stored on your device: Used for automatic volume switching and restoring based on per-app settings, condition rules, and category rules; handling volume changed with the volume keys; pausing; on-screen notices; applying your theme and language; applying Remove Ads; limiting how often ads and review requests appear; and service-off notifications. From version 1.0.28, they are also used to calculate and show the welcome offer period and to send its ending reminder.
  • Firebase Analytics events: Used to understand how features are used, what types of apps volume settings are used for, and where people stop during the first-run guide, accessibility setup, and purchase, in order to improve the app.
  • Crashlytics reports: Used to find the causes of errors and improve app stability.
  • AdMob: Used to show ads so the app can be offered for free.
  • Google Play billing and In-App Review: Used to process and confirm Remove Ads purchases and to let you leave a review for the app if you want to.

6. Data Retention and Deletion

  • Deleting settings: Turning an app off in the app list keeps its setting on your device in the off state; deleting an app on the home screen removes that app's volume setting and its condition rules from your device. Condition rules can also be deleted individually on the volume editor screen, and turning a category rule off keeps it on your device in the off state with its value.
  • Temporary baseline volume record: Deleted right after the original volume is restored, or when it can no longer be restored because the output device changed. If the app process stops unexpectedly, the original volume is restored the next time the service connects and the record is then deleted; records older than 24 hours are deleted without restoring. The speaker volume kept for when output returns to the speaker is held only in memory for a short time and is not stored.
  • Uninstalling: Uninstalling the app deletes the volume settings, rules, app settings, and temporary records stored on your device. If you only clear the app's data, your ad consent choice is reset. In versions up to 1.0.27, Remove Ads is activated again by the Google Play lookup the next time you open the app; from version 1.0.28, it is not activated again automatically; it is activated again when you tap Restore purchase on the Remove Ads screen, or tap the buy button with an account found to own the product (no payment is made) (the same applies if you uninstall and reinstall the app; see section 4 for the detailed conditions). From version 1.0.28, the welcome offer period is also calculated anew from the next time you open the app.
  • Backup files: Exported backup files stay where you saved them and are not deleted when you uninstall the app. Delete them yourself if you no longer need them. If you choose a location provided by another service, such as a cloud drive, the file is kept under that service's policies.
