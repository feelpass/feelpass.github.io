Privacy Policy

AppSet - Per-App Settings

In short: AppSet has no account or login. The settings you choose for each app (screen orientation, dimming, keep screen on, media volume, keyboard) and your rules are stored in the app's storage on your device; if Google backup is turned on for your device they may be included in Android Auto Backup, and they are also written to backup files you create yourself. AppSet is not a keyboard (input method), and it does not read, store, or send what is shown on the screen, anything you type with a keyboard, or any key presses. Media volume and the default keyboard are really changed on the device while you use that app and are then changed back; orientation, dimming, and keep screen on are applied with windows shown by AppSet, and your system brightness and rotation settings are not changed. The app shows ads through Google AdMob (no ads are shown while Remove Ads is active; see section 4 for when it becomes active) and sends usage events and crash reports to Google through Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. The names and package names of the apps you set up, which keyboards you chose, and the values you set (orientation, dimming, volume) are not included in usage events. Instead, usage events include the app type assigned by Android (for example, game, video, or social) and which items are turned on (on/off per item), and every time the Accessibility Service detects that an app has come to the foreground and applies its settings, this information is sent to Firebase Analytics.

1. Information We Collect and Process

The information AppSet handles is as follows.

  • Per-app settings and rules — stored on your device: The app stores the following in a database on your device. The "setting values" below are, for each of the five items, whether it is on and its value (orientation mode, dimming %, whether to keep the screen on, media volume %, and the chosen keyboard). The chosen keyboard is stored as the identifier Android gives each keyboard (a string made of the keyboard app's package name and component name).
    • Per-app settings: for each app you set up, its package name and app name, whether the setting is on, the setting values, the time a per-app pause ends, and the times the setting was created and last changed
    • Time rules: for rules you create for an app or a category, their time range (start and end time), setting values, whether the rule is on, the rule order, and the times it was created and last changed
    • Category rules: for each app type (games, video, social, and so on), whether the rule is on, the setting values, and the time it was last changed
    • My categories: the name you entered, whether it is on, the setting values, the package names of its apps, and the times it was created and last changed
    • Apps excluded from categories: the package name of each app you took out of category handling, the time you did so, and the time the exclusion ends (including no end time)
    This data is in the app's storage on your device; if Google backup is turned on for your device it may be included in Android Auto Backup (see section 6), and when you export, it is also written to a backup file (see "Backup files" below). The app does not send this data anywhere.
  • App settings and operating records — stored on your device: The time a global pause ends; your theme and language; whether on-screen notices are shown and their position; whether you finished the first-run guide (onboarding); whether Remove Ads is active (true/false); whether Remove Ads has ever been active on this device (true/false); records for the welcome offer (the time you first opened the app on this device, and whether you dismissed the welcome offer card on the home screen); records used to decide when to ask for a review (the number of times settings were applied, counted only up to 10; the time you first opened the home screen; the time a review was last requested); records used to decide when to send a service-off notification (whether the service has ever connected, the time it last connected, the time a notification was last sent, and whether notification permission has been requested); the sibling app cards you dismissed on the home screen (the package name of the dismissed sibling app — one of AppRotate, AppVolume, or AppKeys); and whether each one-time usage event has already been sent. These are stored in the app's storage on your device, and they too may be included in Android Auto Backup if Google backup is turned on (see section 6).
  • Return-value record — stored on your device: Changing the media volume or the default keyboard really changes the value on the device, so just before changing it, the app saves a single record in the app's storage on your device to use when changing it back: that app's package name, the time it was saved, and, for each item it changes (media volume, default keyboard), the value before you entered the app and the value it changes to (and, when it overwrites an earlier record, the value in place at that moment). Volume is written as a volume step number and the keyboard as its identifier. There is only one such record, and it is deleted once the values have been changed back and handling of that app ends. Orientation, dimming, and keep screen on have no value to change back, so they are not part of this record. If the Accessibility Service stopped without changing the values back, the record is read and handled the next time the service connects, and a record older than 24 hours is deleted without being used. This record is not sent, is not included in the backup files you create, and is excluded from Android Auto Backup and device-to-device transfer.
  • Installed app information — read on your device: So you can choose which apps to set up, the app loads the package name, name, and icon of the apps shown on your home screen (launcher) from your device and displays them. For category rules and for the app type value in usage events, the app also reads the app type Android assigns to each app (ApplicationInfo.category) on your device. The loaded list is not stored or sent, and the app type is sent only as the app_category value of the usage events below; only apps you turn on or whose settings you change, apps you put in one of My categories, apps you exclude from a category, and apps imported from a backup file are saved to the per-app settings and rules above.
  • Keyboard information and media volume — read on your device: To show the keyboards you can choose from and to decide whether a keyboard can be switched to, the app reads from Android the list of keyboards turned on on your device (identifier, name, the keyboard app's package name, and whether it is a keyboard for typing text), the current default keyboard, and the names of the keyboards you saved, and it shows the keyboard apps' icons on screen. It also reads the current media volume, to use as the starting value when you first turn on the volume item on a settings editor screen and so that the Accessibility Service can change the volume and change it back. This information is used on your device for display (selection lists, settings screens, and on-screen notices) and for deciding what to apply, and it is not sent. Only the values you set (the identifier of the keyboard you choose and the volume %; when you first turn an item on, the default keyboard and media volume at that moment are filled in as the starting values) are saved to the per-app settings and rules above, and the values at the time settings are applied are saved to the return-value record above.
  • Whether a sibling app's accessibility service is on — checked on your device: If a sibling app that manages the same value as AppSet (AppRotate: orientation, AppVolume: volume, AppKeys: keyboard) is on at the same time, the two apps can fight over that value. To let you know, each time you open the home screen the app reads from Android the list of accessibility services that are currently on (the package name of the app each service belongs to) and checks only whether the three sibling apps are in it. If a sibling app's service is on and a setting you have turned on in AppSet uses that item, a card is shown on the home screen. The list that was read is not stored and is not sent. What is stored is only the package name of a sibling app whose card you dismissed (see "App settings and operating records" above), and what is sent is only which sibling app's card was shown (one of approtate, appvolume, or appkeys) when a card is shown (see sibling_conflict_shown below). The app does not read, import, or change the sibling apps' settings.
  • Usage events — sent to Firebase Analytics: The app sends the following events to Firebase Analytics. App names and package names; keyboard identifiers, names, and package names; and the values you set (orientation mode, dimming %, volume %) are not included in any event. The "app type" below (app_category) is the value assigned by Android (one of game, audio, video, image, social, news, maps, productivity, accessibility, undefined, other, or unknown), and the "item count" (item_count) is the number of items turned on in that setting (0 to 5).
    • screen_view: when you open a screen — the screen name (home, app list, per-app settings editor, rule editor, settings, theme/language selection, category rules, category settings, category app list, My categories editor, Remove Ads, onboarding)
    • onboarding_page_view, onboarding_skipped, onboarding_completed: when you view a page of the first-run guide, when you end it with "Later", and when you finish it (including with "Later") — the page number and whether the Accessibility Service is on
    • a11y_disclosure_shown, a11y_disclosure_result: when the accessibility disclosure dialog is shown and when you agree or decline — where it was opened (home, settings, onboarding) and your response
    • enable_service_clicked: when you agree to the accessibility disclosure on the home screen or in onboarding and go to the accessibility settings (no additional values)
    • a11y_settings_abandoned: when you agreed to the disclosure and went to the settings but came back within 5 minutes without turning the service on (no additional values)
    • a11y_enabled: once, when the Accessibility Service connects for the first time (no additional values)
    • service_connected: when the Accessibility Service connects — the number of apps whose per-app setting is on
    • a11y_lost: when the Accessibility Service is unbound or destroyed, or when a service-off notification is sent — the reason (unbound, destroyed, detected off)
    • managed_app_added (and first_app_added the first time): when you add a new app or turn an app back on in the app list, or first save a new app's setting on the settings editor screen — that app's app type and where it happened
    • managed_app_removed: when you turn off an app in the app list or delete an app on the home screen — that app's app type and where it happened
    • profile_changed: when a per-app setting you changed on the settings editor screen is saved — that app's app type and the item count. Which items they are and their values are not sent
    • schedule_set: when you save a new time rule on the rule editor screen — what the rule belongs to (an app, an app-type category, or one of My categories) and the item count. The times and the values are not sent
    • category_rule_changed: when you turn a category rule on or off or save a new value — the app type, whether it is on, and the item count. The values are not sent
    • custom_category_saved: when you create or edit and save one of My categories (a group of apps you pick yourself), or turn it on or off or save a new value for it — the number of apps in it, whether it is on, and the item count. The category name, the names and package names of its apps, and the values are not sent
    • custom_category_deleted: when you delete one of My categories (no additional values)
    • category_app_excluded, category_app_included: when you uncheck or re-check an app on the category app list screen and it is saved — the category kind (app-type category or My categories). Which app and which category are not sent
    • profile_applied (and first_profile_applied the first time): when the Accessibility Service actually applies that app's settings (for example, when it detects a foreground app switch, when a pause ends, when the service connects, or when the screen turns on or you unlock the device) — that app's app type, where the values came from (per-app setting, category rule, or My categories), how the foreground app was detected (detect: an accessibility event, a11y, or Usage access, usage), the item count, and whether each item is on (orientation, dim, keep_on, volume, keyboard, each 0 or 1). The orientation mode, dimming %, volume %, and which keyboard it is are not sent, and the event is not sent if nothing changed when you entered the app (the values were already the same and no new window was shown)
    • profile_released: when you leave an app whose settings were applied and its handling ends — what triggered it (leaving to the home screen or another screen where settings are not applied, switching to another app, a pause, a global pause, the screen turning off, the service stopping, or a record left by an earlier run being handled when the service connects) and the result for media volume and for the default keyboard (changed back to the value before you entered, kept the value you changed to, nothing to change back, could not change back, or that item was not used). Which app it is and the values are not sent
    • profile_item_failed: when one of the items that are on could not be applied — the kind of item (orientation, dimming, keep screen on, volume, keyboard) and the reason (orientation cannot be set on this screen, the window could not be shown, the device does not support changing the volume, the chosen keyboard is turned off or removed, the system did not accept the change, the current value could not be read, or the return-value record could not be saved). Which app it is and the values are not sent
    • pause_set, pause_resumed: when you pause or resume applying per-app settings — the scope (global or app), where it happened (home, on-screen notice, Quick Settings tile), and the pause length in minutes (-1 for indefinite)
    • overlay_action: when, on an on-screen notice, you tap the pause button, choose or cancel an item in the pause length list, open the menu, or in the menu choose to open settings, stop managing this app, or exclude it from its category — the action type. Which app it is is not sent

    • backup_exported, backup_imported: when you export settings to a file or import and merge them from a file — the number of per-app settings exported or imported. The number of rules and file names, locations, and contents are not sent.
    • review_prompted: when the app requests the Google Play review dialog (no additional values)
    • User properties: the number of apps whose per-app setting is on (managed_app_count), whether the Accessibility Service has been turned on (a11y_enabled), whether a Remove Ads purchase was completed on this device (premium, recorded only when a purchase completes), and whether the optional Usage access permission is on (usage_access, true/false)

  • Crash reports — sent to Firebase Crashlytics: When the app crashes, error details, device diagnostic information, and a commit hash identifying the app build (git_hash) are sent. See section 4.
  • Advertising data — collected by Google AdMob: To serve ads, the AdMob SDK may collect and use device identifiers such as the advertising ID, your IP address, and ad interaction data.   • Purchase information — handled by Google Play: Remove Ads purchases are handled by the Google Play billing system. The app does not receive or store payment methods such as card numbers, or billing addresses. The product prices it receives from Google Play are used only to show them on screen and to calculate the welcome offer discount and decide whether to show the offer; they are not stored or sent. The only values about purchases stored on your device are whether Remove Ads is active (true/false) and whether it has ever been active on this device (true/false). See section 4.
  • Backup files — saved where you choose: When you export from the settings screen, the app creates a backup file (JSON) in a location you pick yourself with the system file picker. The file contains an app identifier (appset), the file format version, the export time, per-app settings (package name, app name, whether the setting is on, and the setting values), per-app time rules (package name, time range start and end time, setting values, whether the rule is on, and rule order), category rules (app type, whether the rule is on, and setting values), My categories (the name you entered, whether it is on, setting values, and the package names of its apps), the package names of apps you excluded from categories with no end time, and category time rules (the app type or My category name they belong to, time range start and end time, setting values, whether the rule is on, and rule order). 2. Accessibility Service

AppSet uses the Android Accessibility Service for its core feature: when one of your selected apps comes to the foreground, applying the settings you chose for that app (screen orientation, dimming, keep screen on, media volume, keyboard) while it is on screen, and undoing them when you leave. Only the items you turned on for that app are applied. AppSet is not a keyboard (input method) and contains no input method service. The keyboard item only changes which of the keyboards already turned on on your device is the default keyboard.

  • Events it receives: The service subscribes to one event type only: window state change events (TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED). It does not subscribe to text change events or key events. Separately from accessibility events, while the service is connected it also receives Android's screen-off, screen-on, device-unlocked, and device-shutdown broadcasts.
  • What it reads: It reads the package name and window class name from each event, whether that window is full screen, and the package name of the currently active window, to determine which app is in the foreground. The service configuration declares window content retrieval (canRetrieveWindowContent), but the only value the app's code reads from the active window is the package name. It does not read text shown on the screen or anything you type with a keyboard, and it does not store or send them. It does not receive or intercept key presses (it does not declare key event filtering, and there is no code that handles key events). It does not capture the screen.
  • Protected screens and the optional Usage access permission: For some app screens (for example, the compose screen of some mail apps), Android delivers neither events nor the active window to the Accessibility Service, so the service alone cannot tell that the app has come to the foreground. Only if you turn on the optional Usage access permission yourself, the service reads the package name of the one app that most recently came to the foreground from Android's usage records, at the moment it could not read the active window, and uses it to determine the foreground app (see section 3). If you do not turn this permission on, usage records are not read; settings are not applied when you go straight to such a screen, and if you move straight from an app whose settings were applied to such a screen, the previous app's settings stay in place until a readable screen comes to the foreground.
  • What it does — items applied with a window (orientation, dimming, keep screen on): When an app whose per-app setting is on (or an app with no per-app setting that falls under a category rule or My category you turned on) comes to the foreground, the service applies these items by attaching windows that an accessibility service is allowed to show (accessibility overlays). Orientation and keep screen on are set as attributes of an invisible window of zero size, and dimming is a translucent black window that covers the whole screen (including the status bar, navigation bar, and keyboard), at 5 to 90% darkness. Neither window receives touches or key input, so touches go straight through to the screen below, and the app does not read the screen content under them. Your system brightness and rotation settings are not changed for these items. When you leave that app, the windows are removed, and with the windows gone everything is as before. If the Accessibility Service stops, Android removes these windows.
  • What it does — items that change a value on the device (media volume, default keyboard): Media volume is changed with Android's volume function (AudioManager.setStreamVolume, the media stream only), and the default keyboard is changed with the keyboard switching function Android provides to accessibility services (SoftKeyboardController.switchToInputMethod). This really changes the device's media volume and the default keyboard value in Android's system settings, and they stay that way while you use the app. When you leave that app, they are changed back to the values from before you entered. So that it can change them back, the service saves a return-value record on your device before changing anything (see section 1). For the keyboard, if you change to another keyboard yourself while using the app, your choice is kept and is not changed back. For media volume, it is changed back to the volume from before you entered when you leave, even if you changed the volume inside the app. The keyboard can only be switched to keyboards that are turned on on your device, so if the keyboard you set is turned off or removed, the keyboard is not switched. The volume may not reach the value you set if the system limits it (for example, Do Not Disturb or a fixed-volume device). If an item already has that value when you enter the app, that item is not changed.
  • When settings are applied and undone: If one of the items that are on cannot be applied, the other items are still applied. If time rules exist, the rule that matches the device time when you enter the app replaces only the items that rule turns on with the rule's values. When the screen turns off or the device shuts down, the windows are removed and the volume and keyboard are changed back; while the screen is off or the lock screen is showing, nothing is applied; and when you unlock the device the service checks the foreground app again and applies its settings. Nothing is applied to the home screen (launcher), system UI, Android Settings, the package installer, or AppSet itself. If the Accessibility Service is turned off or stopped and cannot change things back, the changed volume and keyboard may remain on the device (the windows are gone); in that case the saved record is handled item by item the next time the service connects (a record older than 24 hours is not used, and an item whose value has changed in the meantime is not changed back). On large-screen devices running Android 16 or later, the orientation cannot be set, so only that item is not applied.
  • On-screen notices: When settings are applied and something changed, the service briefly shows a notice on the screen. This notice is not an Android notification but a window the Accessibility Service draws over the screen (an accessibility overlay), and the window does not receive key input. The notice shows the app name and a summary of the applied items (for example, the orientation, dimming %, volume %, and keyboard name), and any items that could not be applied. It has a pause button and a menu button, whether the settings were applied by a per-app setting, a category rule, or one of My categories. Tapping the pause button shows a list of pause lengths, and for the length you choose (including no end time) settings are not applied for that app only (for an app handled by a category, only that app is taken out of category handling for that time). The menu has open settings, pause, stop managing this app (apps handled by a per-app setting) or exclude from the category (apps handled by a category), and close. When none of the items that are on could be applied, a one-line notice with no buttons is shown; no notice is shown when settings are undone. In the settings screen under "On-screen notices", you can choose to show them or not.
  • What is sent: Each time settings are applied, that app's app type, where the values came from, how the foreground app was detected (an accessibility event or Usage access), the item count, and whether each item is on are sent to Firebase Analytics as a profile_applied event. In addition, the Accessibility Service sends profile_released (what triggered it and the results for volume and keyboard), profile_item_failed (the kind of item that could not be applied and why), service_connected (the number of apps turned on), the user property usage_access (whether Usage access is on), a11y_lost, and, when you use the buttons or menu on an on-screen notice, overlay_action and pause_set events (see section 1). App names and package names, keyboard identifiers and names, and the values you set (orientation mode, dimming %, volume %) are not sent. No profile_applied event is sent when you switch to an app that has neither its own setting to apply nor a category rule turned on, an app whose own setting you have turned off, or an app you have paused.
  • On-device storage: The app's code does not keep a history of foreground app switches. The values the Accessibility Service writes to your device on its own are the single return-value record (see section 1; it contains the package name of the app the volume or keyboard is currently changed for, and it is deleted after changing them back), the clearing of a global pause when its period ends, the settings-applied count used for review requests, the service connection record (whether it has ever connected and the time it last connected), and whether each one-time usage event has already been sent. In addition, when you use the buttons or menu on an on-screen notice, it writes what you did: the time a pause ends (to the app's own setting for an app handled by a per-app setting, or, together with the package name, to the apps excluded from categories for an app handled by a category), turning the per-app setting off, and excluding the app from its category (for an app-type category the package name is added to the apps excluded from categories; for one of My categories the app is removed from its apps). However, events sent to Firebase are temporarily kept on your device by the Firebase SDK until they are sent.
  • Actions on your behalf: The only things the service does on your behalf are showing the windows described above (orientation, dimming, keep screen on), changing the media volume and the default keyboard, and opening AppSet's own screen when you tap "open settings" in the on-screen notice menu. It does not tap, scroll, or type for you. It does not declare the gesture capability (canPerformGestures).
  • Your control: The Accessibility Service works only after you turn it on yourself in Android Settings. Before taking you to the accessibility settings screen, the app shows a disclosure dialog and asks for your consent. If you turn the service on within 5 minutes after agreeing, you are brought back to the app automatically. You can turn the service off at any time in Android Settings, and you can pause applying per-app settings from the home screen, the Quick Settings tile, or an on-screen notice.

3. Permissions

The permissions the app itself declares are the Accessibility Service, Usage access (optional), notifications, internet, and the app, keyboard, and sibling app queries below. The other permissions are declared by libraries bundled with the app.

  • Accessibility Service (BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE): Detects the foreground app, applies the settings you chose for that app, and undoes them (see section 2).
  • Usage access (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS) — optional permission: It is off by default and works only after you read the notice under "More accurate app detection" in the app's settings and turn it on yourself on the Android settings screen. What is read: only when the Accessibility Service could not read the active window, the package name of the one app that most recently came to the foreground, from the last 10 minutes of usage records (to pick that one app, the app goes through the package names and screen class names in the records of screens coming to the foreground and leaving it during those 10 minutes; it is not read periodically, and at most 3 times for one window event; it is not read while the screen is off or locked or while everything is paused). Purpose: to apply the settings you chose for an app on protected screens the Accessibility Service cannot see, and to undo the previous app's settings when you move to such a screen. Storage and transmission: the package name is used on the spot to determine the foreground app and is not stored separately (if that app's volume or keyboard is changed, its package name is part of the single return-value record described in section 1). Your app usage time and usage history are not stored and are not sent anywhere. What is sent is only whether this permission is on (true/false) and, when settings are applied, a marker that the foreground app was detected through this permission (see section 1). How to turn it off: turn AppSet off under Android Settings > Apps > Special app access > Usage access (the name may differ by device), or tap "More accurate app detection" in the app's settings to go to the same screen. Everything else keeps working without it.
  • Installed app, keyboard, and sibling app queries: To show the list of apps you can set up, the app declares that it queries apps shown on your home screen (launcher); to show keyboard names and icons, it declares that it queries the keyboards (input methods, android.view.InputMethod) on your device; and to check whether a sibling app's accessibility service is on, it declares that it queries the three sibling apps (com.coco.approtate, com.coco.appvolume, com.coco.appkeys) (see section 1). Libraries bundled with the app also declare some query entries automatically (the ad SDK: apps that handle web links, phone calls, text messages, and adding calendar events; the Google Play Billing Library: the Google Play Store app). The app does not request the permission to see every installed app (QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES).
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS, Android 13 and later): If the Accessibility Service is turned off, or is on but not connected, the app sends an "AppSet is off" notification. Using WorkManager, the app checks the service state about every 6 hours (whether the service is connected and whether this app is turned on in Android's accessibility settings). The check itself happens on your device, and an a11y_lost event is sent when the notification is sent. It notifies you once each time the service goes off, does not notify again while it stays off, and notifies once more if the service reconnects and then goes off again. Tapping the notification opens the app's home screen. The permission is requested on the home screen while the Accessibility Service is running, or when you come back after turning the service on from the settings screen; it is asked only once across the app and not asked again if you deny it. The app does not send persistent notifications. It sends one kind of promotional notification: a reminder that the welcome offer is ending. When you open the app's screens, it uses WorkManager to schedule one reminder for about 24 hours before the welcome offer period ends (7 days from the time you first opened the app on this device; see section 4). The reminder is not sent if, at that time, Remove Ads is active, a lookup found that this Google account already owns a Remove Ads product, the ownership lookup has not finished, the offer period has ended, the price of the welcome offer product could not be received from Google Play, that price is not lower than the regular product's price, or notification permission is not granted. It is sent on a notification channel separate from the service-off notification ("Offers"), and tapping it opens the app's Remove Ads screen (the app does not go there while the first-run guide is in progress). The app does not ask for notification permission separately for this reminder.
  • Internet (INTERNET): Used to check ad consent and load ads, to send Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics data, and to open this privacy policy page from the settings screen.
  • Permissions declared by libraries: Libraries bundled with the app automatically declare the following permissions.
    • Network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): declared by the Google AdMob SDK, Firebase Analytics, and others, and used to check the network connection state.
    • Advertising ID (AD_ID) and ad services permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS): declared by the Google AdMob SDK and Firebase Analytics for ad serving and measurement.
    • Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING): declared by the Google Play Billing Library and used to buy Remove Ads and to confirm and restore the purchase.
    • WAKE_LOCK and install referrer access (BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE): declared by Firebase Analytics and others. The install referrer is Google Play information about how the app was installed.
    • FOREGROUND_SERVICE and DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION: declared by Android libraries (WorkManager, AndroidX). The app does not run a foreground service, and the latter is a permission private to this app that prevents other apps from calling receivers used only inside the app.
  • Quick Settings tile: You can add a "Per-app settings" tile to Quick Settings in the notification shade. Tapping the tile pauses or resumes applying per-app settings, and opens the app instead when the Accessibility Service is not running (long-pressing the tile also opens the app). The tile does not request any permission (it is protected by BIND_QUICK_SETTINGS_TILE so that only the system can connect to it).
  • Permissions the app does not use: The app does not use the display-over-other-apps permission (SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW), the modify-system-settings permissions (WRITE_SETTINGS, WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS), or Do Not Disturb access. Windows over the screen are shown only as the Accessibility Service's own windows; the device's default keyboard is changed only through the keyboard switching function Android provides to accessibility services; and media volume is changed only through Android's volume function, which needs no separate permission (see section 2). The app contains no input method service and does not declare the input method permission (BIND_INPUT_METHOD).

6. Data Retention and Deletion

  • Deleting settings: Turning an app off in the app list keeps its setting on your device in the off state; deleting an app on the home screen removes that app's setting and its time rules from your device. Time rules can also be deleted individually on the settings editor screen, and turning a category rule off keeps it on your device in the off state with its value.
  • Uninstalling: Uninstalling the app deletes the per-app settings, rules, app settings, and return-value record stored on your device. If the app is uninstalled while the Accessibility Service has the volume or keyboard changed, that value may remain on the device, and you can change it yourself in Android's volume and keyboard settings. If you only clear the app's data, your ad consent choice is reset. Remove Ads is not activated again automatically; it is activated again when you tap Restore purchase on the Remove Ads screen, or tap the buy button with an account found to own the product (no payment is made) (the same applies if you uninstall and reinstall the app, although this can differ as described under "Android Auto Backup" below if app data is restored from a Google backup when you reinstall; see section 4 for the detailed conditions). The welcome offer period is also calculated anew from the next time you open the app.
  • Backup files: Exported backup files stay where you saved them and are not deleted when you uninstall the app. Delete them yourself if you no longer need them. If you choose a location provided by another service, such as a cloud drive, the file is kept under that service's policies.
  • Android Auto Backup: The app allows Android Auto Backup and excludes only the return-value record (section 1) from backup and device-to-device transfer. So if Google backup is turned on for your device, your per-app settings and rules (including the package names and names of the apps you set up, the orientation, dimming, and volume values, and the identifiers of the keyboards you chose) and app settings may be included in your Google Account backup and restored to a new device or to a device where you uninstalled and reinstalled the app. This backup is handled by Android and Google; it is not something the app sends itself. App settings include the Remove Ads records and the welcome offer records (section 1), so on a device where app settings were restored from a backup, Remove Ads may be activated again by the Google Play ownership lookup without tapping Restore purchase, and the welcome offer period may continue from the earlier first-open time. You can manage backups from the Google backup menu in Android Settings.
  • Refunds: If a Remove Ads purchase is refunded or canceled, Remove Ads is deactivated at the next Google Play lookup: ads are shown again and the free usage limits in section 4 apply again (you cannot create new per-app settings, time rules, or My categories beyond the limits, turn on a category rule that is off, or export or import a settings backup). Your per-app settings, rules, and other data on your device are not deleted, and settings, rules, and categories you already created and category rules that are already on keep working.
  • Information sent to Google: Information sent to AdMob, Firebase Analytics, Firebase Crashlytics, and Google Play is retained by Google under each service's policies. Uninstalling the app does not delete information that has already been sent.
  • Stopping transmission: The app has no in-app setting to turn off Firebase Analytics or Crashlytics. Turning off the Accessibility Service stops settings from being applied automatically, and the events the Accessibility Service sends (section 2) are no longer generated. Even with the Accessibility Service turned off, events from using the app's screens and ad requests still occur when you use the app. AdMob SDK initialization and ad requests occur only when you open the app's screens, but when the app process starts, the Google Play purchase lookup and the service state check run, and an a11y_lost event is sent when that check sends a service-off notification.
